Roadway Asset Management Optimization

Multi-objective optimization for integrated roadway asset maintenance and rehabilitation considering traffic dynamics and environmental impacts

Status: Accepted at Transportation Research Record (TRR)

Roadway asset management involves complex trade-offs between agency costs, user costs, environmental impacts, and network-level performance. Traditional single-objective approaches fail to capture this complexity.

This project develops a multi-objective optimization framework for integrated roadway asset maintenance and rehabilitation (M&R) planning that explicitly accounts for:

  • Traffic dynamics: M&R activities cause lane closures and detours, generating user delay costs that vary by timing and location
  • Environmental impacts: Construction activities and vehicle emissions during work zones are incorporated as optimization objectives
  • Network-level coordination: Segment-level decisions are jointly optimized to maximize network performance

Related publications:

  • Meng et al. (2023). Multiobjective optimization method for pavement segment grouping. Journal of Infrastructure Systems, 29(1), 04022047.

The framework enables transportation agencies to make informed, data-driven investment decisions that balance budget constraints against service quality and sustainability goals.
