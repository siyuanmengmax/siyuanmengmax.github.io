02 / Doctoral research framework

Four studies. A connected framework.

My doctoral work connects roadside point clouds to traffic safety analysis, with quality and reliability as recurring questions at every stage.

A shared research concernQuality & reliability: background confidence · detection quality · trajectory continuity · interpretable safety measures
FRGB3DILLUSTRATIVE SCENE
STUDY 01

Separating motion from the scene.

Background modeling provides foreground observations for downstream perception. Toggle the overlay to compare a synthetic point cloud with its foreground view.

THE QUALITY QUESTION

How reliable is the distinction between background and moving objects?

Read FRGB3D ↗
STUDY 02

From points to traffic participants.

Detection organizes foreground points into object hypotheses. This illustration adds colored groups and bounding boxes to the same synthetic road scene.

THE QUALITY QUESTION

How does uneven point coverage affect an object’s representation?

Read MulDet3D ↗
STUDY 03

Maintaining identity over time.

Tracking connects observations across frames. The trails and labels show the idea of continuous vehicle trajectories rather than outputs from the tracking algorithm.

THE QUALITY QUESTION

Can identity and trajectory continuity survive sparse observations and occlusion?

Explore the manuscript list ↓
STUDY 04

Interpreting interactions with care.

Trajectory-based safety analysis depends on the observations behind it. The bands illustrate positional uncertainty around two crossing paths; no TTC, PET, or risk score is computed here.

THE QUALITY QUESTION

How could trajectory uncertainty change the interpretation of a close interaction?

05 / Background

Engineering with a wider view.

Siyuan (Max) Meng

蒙思源

Ph.D., UMass Amherst

I received my Ph.D. in Civil Engineering (Transportation Engineering) from the University of Massachusetts Amherst in September 2026, advised by Dr. Chengbo Ai.

My dissertation, Quality-Aware Multi-LiDAR Framework for Traffic Perception and Safety Analysis, developed a reliability-aware pipeline spanning background modeling, object detection, tracking, and traffic safety analysis.

Before UMass Amherst, I earned my M.S. and B.S. degrees at Chang’an University, where my master’s research was advised by Dr. Qiang Bai. My earlier work focused on pavement maintenance optimization and deep learning-based airport curbside vehicle detection.
